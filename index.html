What it does

It's built for shops with 5 to 50 machines, where the owner walks the floor to find out what's happening.

Live status
Run, hold, alarm, idle and setup for every machineRead straight from the control, so nobody has to fill out a clipboard.
Alarms
Every alarm logged with time and programDowntime has a cause attached to it, which gives maintenance something to work from.
Setup time
Setups detected automatically, confirmed by the operatorYou finally get real setup numbers for quoting and scheduling.
Manager view
Utilization and OEE that match what happened on the floorMetrics come only from data the machines actually reported. No guessed numbers.
Hardware
Runs on one small box on the shop networkIt started on a Raspberry Pi and now runs on an industrial PC. There's no cloud dependency, and your data stays in the building.

Built by the person who fixes the machines

I'm Santiago Melgar, a maintenance technician at a CNC shop in the Bay Area. I work on Fanuc and Mazak controls, servo systems and electrical faults every day.

Shops like ours don't have good visibility into their machines. Enterprise monitoring is priced and scoped for big plants, so I built ShopSync myself and put it on our own floor. It has been running production for months.

Next up: part tracking across the floor, camera-based machine vision, and AI agents that handle front-office work like quoting and purchase orders.
